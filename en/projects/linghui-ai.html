Linghui AI was originally called “AI Expert”. When I joined, it already had a foundation for text-to-image, text-to-video and image-to-image creation. The team wanted to add digital avatars and lip-sync capabilities to make video production easier for online sellers, talking-head creators and individuals with promotional needs.

Problem identification came from competitor analysis and my end-to-end review of the existing product. No formal user interviews or usability tests were conducted.

The homepage should answer:
what can I create here?

The old homepage combined creation tools with general AI chat, blurring its focus. I prioritised avatar customisation and talking-avatar videos, then organised image and video generation. Effects and example projects made possible outputs more tangible.

Capability priorities

Give avatars a clear entry point

Place avatar customisation and talking-avatar videos at the centre, supported by image and video generation.

Understanding the product

Explain possibilities through examples

Show creative effects and other people’s work beneath the tools so users can see what they could produce.

Positioning decisions

Focus on content creation

Adjust the prominence of general chat so the homepage supports the platform’s core creative purpose.

From “all the features are here”
to “I know what to do next”.

The old talking-avatar page combined video, voiceover and generation settings. Sub-features also opened separate workflows, making sequence and progress difficult to judge. I divided production into stages and kept step guidance visible throughout.

Three breaks I found in the original journey

  1. Before starting: no overview

    Users did not know what to prepare or how far they were from completion.

  2. During creation: disconnected subtasks

    Opening voiceover and other sub-features meant completing another workflow, interrupting the main task.

  3. After interruption: work was hard to resume

    Users had to prepare content again, with no clear way to save and reuse outputs from each stage.

Create a reusable avatar from one photograph

Upload a photo and create an avatar, then configure the script and voiceover before generating the video.

Progress percentages and waiting times shown in the designs are illustrative, not measured generation performance.

Explain the first time,
start directly the next time.

I separated the introductory instructions from in-flow step guidance. A first-use page establishes expectations; returning users can skip it while retaining access to instructions when needed.

First creation

Understand, then create

Preview steps and results → Prepare assets → Configure content → Generate

Returning creation

Reuse outputs and start directly

Choose an existing avatar or video → Configure new content → Generate

Make avatars and voiceovers the starting point for the next creation.

I proposed and refined saving outputs at each stage. Generated avatars and voiceovers remain available for later selection and video creation. Once avatar generation begins, users can leave rather than stay on the waiting screen.

Generate an avatar
Save as a reusable asset＋Synthesise a voiceover
Keep generated outputs→Make a video
Combine existing outputs

This saves generated assets. It does not automatically restore every unsubmitted script, style choice or other draft setting.

First-release trade-off

Keep output reuse; defer original upload management.

I proposed upload history and deletion controls. Given development effort and the goal of launching quickly, we deferred those management features. Saving and reusing generated avatars and voiceovers shipped as part of the creation workflow.

Before users generate,
explain cost and eligibility.

Beyond UI design, I helped define membership benefits, credit costs and payment timing, and wrote product documentation. Users need to distinguish two things: how many credits a generation costs, and whether a feature requires membership.

Eligibility

Explain membership requirements early

State the conditions before users enter a member-only feature, rather than revealing the restriction at the end.

Generation cost

Show credit costs on the generate button

Present the required credits before submission, then deduct them according to the rules when generation is requested.

Payment continuity

Return to the existing creation flow

A modal guides membership activation, then returns users to continue generation.

The business rule was that failed generations should not consume credits. Failure feedback and credit explanations were not fully designed at the time and remain areas for improvement.

Keep discovery light,
keep creation focused.

Main feature pages use light backgrounds, while creation workflows use dark backgrounds to distinguish browsing from making. I retained the existing brand colour and adjusted brightness and saturation to maintain recognition and consistency within launch constraints.

Discover and choose

Use content and entry-point hierarchy to help people understand the platform.

Focus on the current creation

Keep the preview and current task prominent, with continuous step guidance.

Deliver a complete experience,
identify what to validate next.

As the sole designer, I made product decisions, worked with engineering through delivery and reviewed the complete creation journey. During QA, I identified and helped resolve issues with back navigation.

From product definition to release

Product definition
Competitor analysis, feature organisation, workflows, membership and credit rules, and product documentation.
Design delivery
Homepage, avatar customisation, talking-avatar videos, effects, membership and credits, account pages, design guidelines and promotional pages.
Engineering collaboration
Scope the first release around development effort, follow implementation and validate the design workflows.
Actual status
The product was released without user promotion; usage mainly came from internal staff. There is not yet enough evidence to assess market performance.
Visual reflection

Reassess the primary colour

Development constraints led us to retain the original primary colour. Looking back, I see room to improve the palette and overall visual direction, particularly its relationship with creative content.

Workflow reflection

Remove more unnecessary steps

Step-by-step guidance established a basic path, but the flow can be simpler. Observing real tasks could reveal steps to combine or choices to reduce through defaults.

Experience gaps

Complete failure handling and asset management

Improve failure feedback and credit explanations, then reassess priorities for upload history and deletion controls.

Help people understand, complete,
and carry their work into the next creation.
